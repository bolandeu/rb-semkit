
 Usage: semkit read {IN} text-stats [OPTIONS]

 The usual SEO metrics of each text, the same numbers each run: a metric column per metric, a row
 per text.

 A cell is a text; an empty cell gives zeros and an empty top. The metric columns are added right
 after the column of the texts.

 The words are those of split-text: punctuation at the edges of a word is dropped, a hyphen or an
 apostrophe inside a word stays («кресло-кровать» is one word), a number with inner points, commas
 or hyphens is one word («31.03.2026»); case and ё are folded. Stopwords are the bundled list of
 the n-grams. Lemmas are the words that are no stopwords. A single-letter word, an abbreviation (г,
 ул, руб, шт, кв, стр…) and a unit (рублей, см, мм, мл, кг) of the bundled word rules are words but
 no lemmas for top, nausea, ttr, mtld and zipf.
 no_digits: leave out the words with a digit («2026», «15000», «A4») of every count, the words
 total included, as if the text did not hold them; chars and chars_no_spaces stay those of the text
 as given.
 exclude: words to leave out the same way, as written, with case and ё folded, not by lemma («года»
 does not leave out «год»).

 The columns, in this order:
 - chars: all characters of the text; chars_no_spaces: the same without whitespace;
 - words: the words of the text, stopwords, abbreviations and units included;
 - unique: distinct lemmas;
 - water: stopwords / words, 0..1;
 - nausea_classic: the square root of the count of the most frequent lemma (the top), 2 decimals;
 - nausea_academic: that count / words, 0..1;
 - top: that lemma; on a tie, the first in the text;
 - ttr: distinct lemmas / lemmas, 0..1;
 - mtld: Measure of Textual Lexical Diversity (McCarthy and Jarvis 2010), threshold 0.72: read the
 lemmas forward, each time the running type/token ratio falls to 0.72 or below, count a factor and
 restart; the last partial run adds (1 - ttr) / (1 - 0.72); do it backward too; mtld = lemmas /
 mean of the two factor counts, 2 decimals; 0 for under 2 lemmas;
 - zipf: R² x 100, 1 decimal, of the linear regression of log count on log rank over the lemma
 counts (rank 1 is the biggest count), so f(r) = C / r^s fits; 0 for under 5 distinct lemmas.

 No norms and no verdicts: the caller sets them.

 The step reads the working column and adds the metric columns right after it; the rows stay.

 Example: semkit read core.csv text-stats write out.csv
